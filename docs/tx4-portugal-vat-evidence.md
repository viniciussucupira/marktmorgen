## Current legal route and decision boundary

[Law 47/2020 of 24 August](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/47-2020-140950562), checked 2026-11-05 in the official Diário da República, adopted the current Portuguese special regimes for services to non-taxable persons, distance sales and certain domestic supplies. Article 2 makes the regimes optional and requires electronic registration with the Portuguese Tax and Customs Authority when an eligible person chooses one.

The Tax Authority's current [consolidated special-regime text](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/civa_rep/Pages/C-IVA-RegEspecial_Lei47_2020.aspx), checked 2026-11-05, defines the non-Union regime in Articles 14 to 18. A person without a business establishment, fixed establishment or domicile in the Union who supplies services to non-taxable persons established or domiciled in the Union may choose Portugal as Member State of identification. Once chosen, the regime applies to all services supplied on those conditions in the Union, regardless of the Member State of taxation.

The official [Circular Letter 30240/2021](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/legislacao/instrucoes_administrativas/Documents/Oficio_circulado_%2030240_2021.pdf), checked 2026-11-05, describes the 1 July 2021 e-commerce transition and confirms that the non-Union regime covers all services supplied by a person not established in the Union to non-taxable persons when those services are located in a Member State.

Older Portuguese MOSS FAQ pages still describe only telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services. They are historical guidance and do not replace the wider current Law 47/2020 scope.

Those sources state conditions, not findings about Marktmorgen. The evidence does not determine that the LLC is outside the Union, lacks a fixed establishment there, supplies a non-taxable person, makes a supply located in Portugal or should choose Portugal as Member State of identification. It does not select a place of supply, registration or service classification. Ordinary Portuguese VAT registration and the non-Union OSS route are not interchangeable defaults.

## Return and deadline qualifications

Article 7 of the Law 47/2020 regime requires an electronic VAT return even when no covered operation occurred in any Member State during the period. A zero return is not evidence of zero preparation, review, submission or payment cost.

For the non-Union regime, Article 18 requires the return by the end of the following month after each calendar quarter to which the services relate.

The Tax Authority-hosted [OSS guide](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/apoio_contribuinte/Comercio_eletronico/Orientacoes/Documents/OSS_guidelines_pt_1.pdf), checked 2026-11-05, lists the same calendar-quarter cycle: Q1 to 30 April, Q2 to 31 July, Q3 to 31 October and Q4 to 31 January. It states that the deadline does not extend when it falls on a weekend or national holiday. These dates are scheme evidence, not a calendar assigned to the LLC.

Once the non-Union regime is chosen, all covered services in the Union are included. This evidence does not make the scheme applicable to any Marktmorgen transaction and does not create a filing date.

## Payment, currency and operation-record boundaries

Article 6 of the Law 47/2020 regime states that a person registered in Portugal pays the tax for each return by the return deadline. The payment carries the declaration reference and is made by bank transfer to the designated account in euro. When consideration is not expressed in euro, the law uses the European Central Bank exchange rate for the last day of the period or, if none was published that day, the next publication day.

Those are conditional mechanics. This document does not calculate tax, choose an exchange implementation or bank, create a deadline, or authorize filing or payment.

Article 7 requires records of covered operations adequate to determine and audit the tax. The OSS guide describes the detailed information, including Member State of consumption, service type and date, taxable amount and currency, later adjustments, VAT rate and amount, payments and advances, invoice information when issued, customer-location evidence and evidence of returns.

The guide requires special-scheme records to be kept for ten years from the end of the year in which the transaction occurred and made available electronically on request to the Member State of identification or consumption. This is a special-scheme evidence minimum. It is not a universal ten-year deletion rule, does not shorten a longer duty or legal hold, and does not change retention, storage location or deletion code.
